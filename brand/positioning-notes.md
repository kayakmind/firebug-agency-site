## Messaging platform

### Section 1: positioning and onliness (approved 2026-10-08)

**Positioning statement (internal, never published verbatim):**

Firebug is a boutique brand and communications agency for organizations whose ambition is
ahead of their presence. Clients work directly with a senior brand and marketing executive,
the kind of person who usually only shows up at a big agency's pitch meeting, who then
actually does the work. Behind that person is an AI-enabled production capability that lets a
small team deliver at a speed and range no agency could offer before. The result is top-tier
strategy and creative, boutique attention, and timelines that used to be impossible:
identity, messaging, content, social, and PR from one team, one standard.

**Onliness statement:** Firebug is the only agency where a senior brand and marketing
executive works with you directly and delivers top-tier creative at a speed no agency has
matched, because the hands-on expertise is human and the heavy lifting isn't.

**The three-way claim:** boutique attention + big-agency range and polish + speed neither
can match, because one experienced person directs technology that does what junior layers
used to.

**Rules out:** production shops (fast and cheap, no strategist), big agencies (the senior
person vanishes after the pitch), strategy consultancies (deck, handshake, gone), platforms
(you never talk to the software).

**Phrases to avoid:** "best of both worlds" (keep the idea, never the words). "Outgrown"
(excludes startups; use "ambition ahead of presence").

### Section 3: core messages (approved 2026-10-08)

Three pillars, mapping to boutique / range / speed and value. Each is a benefit and quietly
answers a fear ("will I get the B team?" / "will it hang together?" / "what's the catch on
fast?"). Cost is folded into pillar 3 so it reads as a consequence of how we work, not a
discount.

**1. You get the senior person. Actually.**
At most agencies, the person who impresses you in the pitch hands you off the week after. At
Firebug, the senior brand strategist you meet on the first call leads the work on the last
day. Every decision about your brand is made by someone who has run brand and communications
for real organizations, and who answers your email.
Client meaning: better judgment on every call, no translation loss, no managing the agency.
Proof slot: About page (Chris, named), engagement model, eventually a client quote on access.

**2. One mind, the whole picture.**
Identity, messaging, content, social, and PR are usually five vendors and five opinions. Here
they come from one team with one standard, so the brand says the same thing in the logo, the
headline, the post, and the press release. That coherence is most of what people mean when
they say a brand feels "put together."
Client meaning: fewer handoffs, nothing lost between brand and channel, an impression that
compounds.
Proof slot: work shown as whole systems (identity through launch), not single deliverables.

**3. Superhuman speed, without the usual trade.**
Speed in this business has always meant one of two things: a template, or a bigger invoice.
Firebug moves at a pace that used to take a department, and the quality doesn't move with it.
That means more options to choose from, more rounds when you want them, and work on your
calendar when you need it, at a cost that makes sense.
Client meaning: the launch date holds, the budget stretches, "another direction?" is a yes.
Proof slot: real timelines from real engagements; a process page from first call to launch.

## Reference: what the market calls "we build your marketing capability" (2026-10-11)

Searched before naming the Marketing systems group. Five vocabularies compete:

- **In-housing / in-house agency.** The industry's term for brands bringing agency work
  inside. ANA 2023: 82% of major brands have in-house agencies (42% in 2008). AAR and Deloitte
  Digital sell "in-housing consultancy." Right idea; jargon, and overshoots a small client.
- **Marketing operations (MarOps, RevOps).** Established, enterprise B2B, means martech
  plumbing (automation platforms, CRM, attribution). Pedowitz Group, TripleDart. Not our register.
- **Marketing operating system / "marketing OS."** The 2026 phrase. MarTech's seven layers
  (workflow, data, content and assets, governance, agents, activation, measurement). A firm
  called Digital.Marketing launched "AI Marketing OS" in October 2026, pitched as reducing
  agency dependency. Crowded and software-flavored.
- **Build, operate, transfer.** Outsourcing-industry model; maps exactly to our three modes.
  Useful as the internal frame, too cold to say.
- **Engines** (content, growth, revenue). Generic.

The finding that shaped the offer: 41% of CMOs who in-housed plan to re-outsource, so the
market lands on hybrid. "Or both" leads.

## Site structure patterns (from Think, Riotters, DATT, Ogilvy; 2026-10-09)

Every site answers four questions in order: what you do, who it's for, why believe you, how to
start. Services get a plain page, grouped in threes to fives, each group with one belief line
and a short list. Think's "If this is you, we can help" pain-point lists = our four moments.
A free, bounded first step (named, timed, "no pitch deck") beats "contact us." Proof is
whatever you honestly have (numbers, case studies, awards, a news feed); nobody fakes the
kind they lack.

Firebug's site needs: a What we do page with grouped disciplines (we have moments, not
groups), a named free first step, one honest form of proof.

**The repeated word.** DATT runs "hold" through hero, every group tagline, and the closer; it
works because it's a verb, not an image. Firebug's equivalent is "impression," not "fire."
"Make an impression" can recur with intent (hero close, a services belief line, end of every
page); fire stays visual and keeps the one-per-screen rule.

**Organizing idea for What we do:** "One story, told in every language" (our "Borderless
Creativity").

**Neighbors on the promise:** DATT "impossible to ignore," Ogilvy "make an impact." Neither
sells time. Speed stays the open lane.
