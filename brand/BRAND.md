# Firebug Agency Brand Guidelines

Reference for positioning, messaging, voice, color, typography, and logo. Source of truth for every place the brand appears: site, LinkedIn, proposals, email. Edit this file rather than letting any other doc drift. The record of how the messaging platform was decided is in `positioning-notes.md`.

Repositioned October 2026. Firebug was an AI consultancy ("Your AI person", Find/Build/Stay). It is now a creative, digital and content agency. AI is how we work, not what we sell, and it is not named on the public site, with one deliberate exception: the Marketing systems group, where the technology is the product (see Voice, rule 1).

**Name.** Firebug Agency. "Firebug" alone in the wordmark and in conversation.

**Domain.** firebugagency.com

**Contact.** kayakmind@gmail.com (site copy uses hello@firebugagency.com as a placeholder; decision pending)

## Positioning

**Internal statement.** Never published verbatim; everything public is derived from it.

Firebug is a boutique creative, digital and content agency for organizations whose ambition is ahead of their presence. Clients work directly with a senior brand and marketing executive, the kind of person who usually only shows up at a big agency's pitch meeting, who then actually does the work. Behind that person is an AI-enabled production capability that lets a small team deliver at a speed and range no agency could offer before. The result is top-tier strategy and creative, boutique attention, and timelines that used to be impossible: brand, design, web, content, and social from one team, one standard.

**Onliness.** Firebug is the only agency where a senior brand and marketing executive works with you directly and delivers top-tier creative at a speed no agency has matched, because the hands-on expertise is human and the heavy lifting isn't.

**The three-way claim.** Boutique attention, big-agency range and polish, and a speed neither can match, because one experienced person directs technology that does what junior layers used to.

**Who it's for.** Deliberately roomy: nonprofits and for-profits, founders, small companies, mid-size organizations, in-house teams. Defined by the moment (a launch, a rebrand, something to announce, a team stretched past what it can make), never by sector or size.

**Rules out.** Production shops (fast and cheap, no strategist). Big agencies (the senior person vanishes after the pitch). Strategy consultancies (deck, handshake, gone). Platforms (you never talk to the software).

## What we do

### The three levels (organizing frame)

Not a sequence. A brand exists at three levels; we work at all three; the client sets the priority.

| Level | What it is | Groups |
|---|---|---|
| **The brand** | Who you are, how you look and sound | Strategy · Identity and design |
| **The story** | The words, images, and pages that carry it | Content · the website as the story's home |
| **The reach** | The system that gets the story out, every channel, every week | Digital platforms, templates, design systems · Social · Newsletters · PR, quietly · Marketing systems (built, run, or both) |

> **The brand. The story. The reach.**
> A brand has to exist at three levels: who you are, what you say, and how far it travels. Most agencies live at one. We work at all three, which is why the identity fits the website, the content sounds like the brand, and the channels say the same thing on the same day.
>
> We can take on all three, or start where you need us most. Some clients want the whole thing built. Many have a brand they're proud of and need the story told, or a story that's ready and needs to travel further. Whatever we make will fit what you already have.

Never "first/then" language. Never "gap." "Reach" in headlines, "system" inside the group copy.

### The four moments (home page tiles, full copy on What we do)

**Something new is coming.** A company, a product, an initiative, a season. It needs a name people remember, a story people repeat, and a launch people notice. We build the brand and then put it in front of the right people, on the day it matters. *Underneath: brand strategy, identity, messaging, launch content, social.* *You walk away with: a brand that exists, and a launch that landed.*

**You've changed. Your brand hasn't.** The work got better. The ambition got bigger. The logo, the language, and the website are still telling the old story. We bring how you look and sound up to who you've become, without losing what people already trust. *Underneath: brand audit, repositioning, identity refresh, messaging, website.* *You walk away with: a brand that matches your reality, and a plan for rolling it out.*

**You have something to say.** A funding round, a report, a milestone, a point of view the field needs to hear. We turn it into a story and get it told: to your audience, across every channel you have, in one consistent voice. *Underneath: campaign strategy, content, social, email, executive thought leadership. PR when the moment calls for it.* *You walk away with: attention, and a reputation that's a notch higher than it was.*

**Your team needs more than it can make.** You have the strategy and the standards. You don't have the hours. We work as an extension of your team, producing on-brand content, social, and digital at a pace that keeps up with your calendar, and we answer to the person who already owns the brand. *Underneath: ongoing content and social, creative direction, brand governance, and a marketing system of your own: built, run, or both.* *You walk away with: a team that ships, and a brand that stays itself while it does.*

> **Build it. Run it. Or both.**
>

> **Or both.** We build it, run it until your team is ready, and hand it over then.

"Technology" and "AI" are allowed in this group's copy, once each, descriptively. The shortened belief line for the Home range block is **Powered by AI. Run by you. Proof point: the senior strategist has designed and run content and production systems for real publishing brands (About, "Who you'll work with"). Fulfilment note: the technology inside a client's system is whatever fits the client; Firebug is tool-agnostic in public and names tools in proposals.

**Public relations** is offered, not forefronted: last item in the footer list, "PR when the moment calls for it" in moment three. PR strategy · Media relations · Press materials · Announcements · Executive visibility.

**Footer discipline list.** Brand and marketing strategy · Identity and design · Digital · Content marketing · Social media · Marketing systems · Public relations

## Beliefs (About page)

Opener: *"The mind is not a vessel to be filled, but a fire to be kindled."* Plutarch, *On Listening to Lectures*. Credit Plutarch; it is often misattributed to Yeats or Socrates.

**Great ideas come from people.** The spark, the judgment, the taste: those are human, and they always will be. Everything after the idea is production, and production should be fast.

**Fast is a form of respect.** Your calendar is real. A launch date, a board meeting, a news cycle. Work that arrives after the moment didn't help, however good it was.

**A brand is one thing, said everywhere.** The logo, the headline, the post, and the press release should sound like they came from the same mind. Usually they didn't. At Firebug they do.

**The senior person does the work.** Not oversees. Does. The strategist you meet on the first call makes the decisions on the last day, and answers your email in between.

**Who you'll work with.** Firebug speaks as "we." Chris Emery is named on the About page only, in third person, four sentences: current role and the organizations it covers; background in communications strategy, science journalism, and running brand and comms for a research institution; the systems side (has designed and run content and production systems for real publishing brands, which is where the speed comes from, and the proof point for Marketing systems); optionally why Firebug exists. ## Voice

Firebug speaks as **we**. The brand, not a person's name. Emails come from Firebug, proposals say Firebug. Chris appears in third person on About, nowhere else on the public site.

**Confident with a spark.** Composed and credible by default. The edge is rare and deliberate.

| Trait | What it means |
|---|---|
| Confident | We say what we think and we don't hedge. No "we believe we can help." We can. |
| Warm | We're on the client's side of the table. "You" appears more than "we." |
| Sharp | Every sentence earns its place. If a line could be cut and nothing is lost, cut it. |
| A spark | Shows up in headlines and last lines, never in body copy about process, pricing, or scope. Two or three per page, not twenty. The occasional dig is at the industry, never at a client or a named competitor. |

**Rules.**

1. Name the benefit, not the mechanism. "Superhuman speed," never "AI-powered." AI, automation, and tool names stay off public pages; they belong in the sales conversation and in proposals, when asked. **One exception, decided 2026-10-11:** in the Marketing systems group, the technology is the product, so "AI" is named plainly, once, in the belief line and once in the group copy on What we do. That belief line is the only place AI appears on Home. The hero, the claim, the argument, About and every other group keep the rule.
2. One fire metaphor per screen. The logo and hero image are already fire. "Light a fire" has the home page's one verbal reference.
3. Fast is a fact, not an adjective. Claim speed with specifics when we have them ("a full identity in three weeks") and with consequences when we don't ("the work on your calendar when you need it"). Never "lightning-fast," "rapid," "agile."
4. Fast never sounds cheap. Cost appears as a consequence of how we work ("at a cost that makes sense"), never as a discount, a price, or "affordable."
5. Services are outcomes. The menu reads as the client's moment or result; disciplines are listed plainly, lower down.
6. "We tell your story" is allowed: singular, second person, the client's. Never the noun "storytelling."
7. Short sentences. Contractions are fine. No em dashes. Sentence case for headlines. Uppercase with tracking is reserved for labels, buttons, and the wordmark.

**Words we use.** Senior, boutique, pace, calendar, impression, standard, one team, taste, judgment, the work, ambition, presence, moment, story.

**Words we avoid.**

| Avoid | Why |
|---|---|
| AI-powered, AI-enabled, AI-first, automation | Mechanism, not benefit. Reads as platform. Exception: the Marketing systems belief line and group copy, where the mechanism is the product |
| Best of both worlds, hybrid | Every hybrid-anything says it |
| Leverage, synergy, solutions, ecosystem, journey, storytelling | Agency fog |
| Cutting-edge, innovative, disruptive, next-level | Claims that disprove themselves by being made |
| Cheap, affordable, budget-friendly | Pulls "fast" toward "low-end" |
| Passionate, obsessed, love what we do | About us, not the client |
| Outgrown (of the client), gap | Diagnoses the client; excludes startups |
| Spark, ignite, blaze, light it up (in body copy) | One fire reference per screen |

**The test for any new line.** Read it twice: once as a nonprofit communications director, once as a founder who just raised. If either one winces, rewrite. If neither would remember it, rewrite.

## Color

All values are exact. Do not substitute Tailwind defaults. The identity direction is called Studio: white ground, ink on a surface, one hot thing per screen.

### Core

| Role | Hex | CSS token | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| White | `#FFFFFF` | `--white` | Page background. The ground everything sits on. |
| Soot | `#1C1A1F` | `--soot` | Ink. Headlines, the single dark band, the one-color mark. Near-black with a faint warm-violet cast; reads as ink, not navy. |
| Vermilion | `#F2401C` | `--vermilion` | The accent. Buttons, "BUG" in the wordmark, the one emphasized word in a Display or H1 line, links, the outer flame. |

### Neutrals (generated from Soot)

| Role | Hex | CSS token | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Paper | `#F5F4F6` | `--paper` | Footer, form fields, the quietest tint. |
| Hairline | `#E4E2E6` | `--hairline` | Rules, borders, dividers. |
| Ash | `#BDB9C1` | `--ash` | Disabled text, the stick on Soot grounds, secondary text on Soot. |
| Smoke | `#8E8A91` | `--smoke` | Captions, labels, the footer, the match stick. |
| Body | `#4F4B54` | `--body` | Body copy. |

### Flame only

| Part | Hex | CSS token |
|---|---|---|
| Outer flame | Vermilion `#F2401C` | `--vermilion` |
| Inner flame | Amber `#FFAA3B` | `--amber` |
| Core | `#FFE4A0` | `--core` |

Amber and Core appear nowhere but inside the flame. Amber may double as the warning color.

### Functional

Success `#1F9D55` · Error `#D92D20` · Warning `#FFAA3B`. Forms and system messages only; never decorative.

### Rules

- **Proportion.** About 85 white, 12 ink, 3 vermilion on any page.
- **Vermilion budget.** Home: four touches (wordmark, the hero word, the button, the closing word). Every other page: two or three. Never two vermilion elements touching.
- **One dark band per page, optional.** Home carries the argument in it; About carries the beliefs. Other pages have none. Text on Soot is white for headlines, Ash for body.
- **No gradients, glows, or shadows.** Ink on a surface.
- **Imagery** sits on white with no frame, or bleeds off the edge.

## Typography

Three faces, each with one job. All load from Google Fonts.

| Face | Weights | CSS token | Used for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bricolage Grotesque | 500, 700, 800 (variable, optical size 12 to 96) | `--font-display` | Display, headlines, claims. Anything that should feel deliberate. |
| Inter | 400, 500, 600 | `--font-sans` | Lead, body, small. Anything that should feel easy to read. |
| Space Mono | 400, 700 | `--font-mono` | Wordmark, labels, buttons, footer. Identity roles only; never a headline. |

### Scale

| Role | Face | Weight | Phone | Desktop | Line | Tracking | Color |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Display | Bricolage | 800 | 64 | 112 | 0.95 | -0.03em, opsz 96 | Soot, one word Vermilion allowed |
| H1 | Bricolage | 800 | 40 | 64 | 1.0 | -0.025em | Soot, one word Vermilion allowed |
| H2 | Bricolage | 800 | 28 | 40 | 1.05 | -0.02em | Soot only |
| H3 | Bricolage | 700 | 19 | 22 | 1.15 | -0.015em, opsz 48 | Soot only |
| Claim | Bricolage | 500 | 19 | 22 | 1.3 | -0.01em, opsz 24 | Soot |
| Lead | Inter | 400 | 17 | 19 | 1.55 | 0 | Soot |
| Body | Inter | 400 | 15 | 17 | 1.65 | 0 | Body `#4F4B54` |
| Small | Inter | 400 | 13 | 14 | 1.55 | 0 | Body |
| Label | Space Mono | 400 | 11 | 11 | 1.4 | 0.3em, uppercase | Vermilion |
| Button | Space Mono | 400 | 12 | 13 | 1 | 0.14em, uppercase | White on Vermilion; Soot outline for secondary |
| Footer | Space Mono | 400 | 11 | 11 | 1.4 | 0.12em, uppercase | Smoke |

### Rules

- Display is reserved for the hero and the closing line. Nothing else gets it.
- Headlines are sentence case. Mono is always uppercase and tracked, and never sets more than four words.
- Body never exceeds 70 characters a line on desktop.
- On a phone the hero runs text first (tagline, claim, lead, button), image second.

### Button

Vermilion fill, white Space Mono text, 6px radius, 14px by 22px padding on a phone. Secondary: 2px Soot outline, Soot text. Hover darkens the fill; nothing glows.

## Layout and devices

### Grid

| | Columns | Gutter | Margin | Max width |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Phone | 4 | 16px | 20px | |
| Desktop | 12 | 24px | 48px | 1200px |

Running text never spans more than five desktop columns (the 70-character line). The hero image takes seven and bleeds off the right edge. Sections sit 96px apart on desktop, 64px on a phone, with a hairline between them. Spacing scale, base 4: 4, 8, 12, 16, 24, 32, 48, 64, 96, 128.

### Devices

The site is drawn with lines, numbers, and words. No icons, no cards, no decoration.

| Device | Spec | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Hairline | 1px `#E4E2E6` | Section rules, dividers, borders, fields. |
| The ground | 1.5px Soot, once per page, under the hero, with an ember at its right end | The line the fire sits on. The only rule in Soot. |
| The ember | 7px Vermilion dot | Active nav item, current step, the one live item in a list. The only icon in the system. |
| Label | Space Mono 11, 0.3em, uppercase, Vermilion | Opens a section. |
| Number | Space Mono, two digits, Vermilion | Items in a series. Replaces pictograms. |
| The hot word | One Vermilion word, always the last word of a Display or H1 line | Never a phrase, never two in a line, never H2 or below. |
| The band | Full bleed Soot, white headline, Ash body, one per page at most | Home: the argument. About: the beliefs. Never holds an image; no vermilion inside except a button. |
| Buttons | Primary: Vermilion fill, white mono. Secondary: 2px Soot outline. Tertiary: mono with a 2px Vermilion underline | Three, no more. |
| Links | Ink text, 1.5px Vermilion underline, 3px offset | In running text. |
| Fields | Single hairline below, Soot; 2px Vermilion on focus; mono label above | Forms ask for the client's moment, not a service. |
| Images | On white, no frame; bleed one edge or align to the grid with a hairline above and below; or a pull quote instead | Photographs of the work. The hero figure is the only illustration. |

**Don't.** Icons for services. Cards with shadows or fills. Flame shapes as decoration. Vermilion fields behind text. Two bands. Gradients, glows, blur. Mono for a sentence.

## Logo

An upright match, already lit. Stick and flame, **no head**: the flame floats at the tip of the stick, which reads as a match that has caught rather than one waiting to be struck. Three flame colors outer to core (Vermilion, Amber, Core) and a Smoke stick.

The flame's width is the unit **x**. Flame height is 1.8x, stick length 3.5x, stick width 0.23x. Everything in the system is measured in x.

### Wordmark

Space Mono Bold, uppercase, tracked 0.18em. "FIRE" takes the text color, "BUG" is always Vermilion. In one-color versions the whole word is ink or white. "Agency" appears only where the full or legal name is needed (footer, documents), in the regular weight in Smoke, never in the lockup. The files have the wordmark outlined to paths, so it renders without the font installed.

### Lockups

| Lockup | Construction | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Horizontal (primary) | Mark three cap heights tall, half an x from the wordmark, baseline on the stick's foot | Nav, email, documents |
| Stacked | Mark centered above the wordmark, 0.4x gap | Covers, cards, social headers, end frames |
| Mark alone | Centered at 65% of the frame's height | Avatar, favicon, app icon, watermark |

### Color versions

| Version | Stick | Flame | Wordmark | Use |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Full color on white | Smoke | Vermilion, Amber, Core | Soot, BUG Vermilion | The default. Almost every use. |
| Full color on Soot | Ash | Vermilion, Amber, Core | White, BUG Vermilion | The dark band, avatars, dark decks. |
| One color, ink | Soot | Soot | Soot | Engraving, stamps, fax, embossing. |
| One color, white | White | White | White | On a Vermilion field or over photography. The only time the mark is all white. |

The flame never recolors. Only the stick and the wordmark adapt to the ground.

### Rules

- **Clear space.** 1x on every side.
- **Minimum size.** Horizontal lockup 24px or 8mm tall. Stacked 44px or 14mm. Mark alone 16px or 5mm.
- **Small marks simplify.** Below 32px the Core drops out. Below 20px the flame is one color. The favicon is the flame alone, no stick.
- **Do not** bring back the head or the old coral, recolor the flame, tilt, stretch, add glow, shadow or gradient, set the wordmark in another face, place the mark inside a sentence, or box it on photography.
