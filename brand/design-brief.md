# Firebug website: design brief

For Claude Design. October 2026. Brand guidelines v1.1.

Everything below has been decided and approved. The brief asks for design within the system, not a new system. Where it says "explore," explore. Where it says "fixed," don't.

---

## 1. The job

Design the four pages of firebugagency.com, phone and desktop, in the Firebug identity. The pages are wireframed and the copy is written. What's missing is the design: the hero figure and its motion, the proportion and rhythm of each page, and the feel of the whole thing when it's real instead of a wireframe.

The site replaces a live one that says something else ("Your AI person"). It will be built in Next.js from a code repository, with the brand tokens as code, so every decision has to be expressible in the system below.

**Who it's for.** Organizations whose ambition is ahead of their presence: founders, nonprofits, small and mid-size companies, in-house teams. Defined by a moment (a launch, a rebrand, something to announce, a stretched team), never by sector. The two readers to test every screen against: a nonprofit communications director, and a founder who just raised. If either winces, it's wrong.

**What success looks like.** A visitor knows in one screen what Firebug is, believes "fast and good" by the second, finds their own situation on the page, and asks for the conversation. The site itself is the first proof point: it has to look like the work of a senior person with taste, delivered fast.

---

## 2. The brand in one page (fixed)

Firebug is a boutique creative, digital and content agency. A senior brand and marketing strategist works with you directly and does the work. Behind that person is a production capability that lets a small team deliver at a speed and range no agency could offer before.

**Onliness.** The only agency where a senior strategist works with you directly and delivers top-tier creative at a speed no agency has matched, because the expertise is human and the heavy lifting isn't.

**The headline stack.**

| Line | Role |
|---|---|
| Light a fire. | Tagline. Hero. |
| Human creativity at superhuman speed. | The claim, under the tagline. |
| Fast and good. Pick both. | The one argument on Home. |
| Make an impression. | The promise. Closes every page. |

**Voice.** Confident, warm, sharp, with a spark in headlines and last lines only. Short sentences. Sentence case. No em dashes. "We," never a person's name except Chris on About, in third person.

**The AI rule.** AI is how Firebug works, not what it sells. It is never named on the site, with one exception: the Marketing systems discipline, where the technology is the product. There it's named once, plainly. Nowhere else. Don't illustrate it, hint at it, or add robot, circuit, or "intelligent" imagery anywhere.

**Fire.** One fire reference per screen. The logo and the hero figure are already fire. Don't add flames, sparks, embers, glows or gradients as decoration.

**Words to avoid.** AI-powered, automation, hybrid, best of both worlds, leverage, synergy, solutions, journey, storytelling, cutting-edge, innovative, disruptive, cheap, affordable, passionate, outgrown, gap, spark/ignite in body copy.

## 3. The identity system (fixed)

Four principles: **Heat, not warmth. Ink on a surface. One hot thing per screen. Senior, not slick.**

**Color.** White ground, Soot ink, Vermilion accent. Proportion on any page: about 85 white, 12 ink, 3 vermilion.

| Token | Hex | Use |
|---|---|---|
| White | #FFFFFF | Ground |
| Paper | #F5F4F6 | Footer, image placeholders, tints |
| Hairline | #E4E2E6 | Rules |
| Ash | #BDB9C1 | Body text on Soot |
| Smoke | #8E8A91 | Captions, the match stick |
| Body | #4F4B54 | Body text on white |
| Soot | #1C1A1F | Ink, the dark band |
| Vermilion | #F2401C | The one accent |
| Amber / Core | #FFAA3B / #FFE4A0 | Inside the flame only |

Vermilion budget: Home four touches, inner pages two or three. A touch is a hot word, a filled button, the ember, or a focus state. Small labels and two-digit numbers are vermilion by rule and don't count.

**Type.** Headlines Bricolage Grotesque 800, optical size 96, tight tracking. Body Inter. Labels, buttons, wordmark and footer in Space Mono.

| Style | Phone / desktop | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Display | 64 / 112 | Hero only. Last word vermilion. |
| H1 | 40 / 64 | Inner page heroes. Last word vermilion. |
| H2 | 28 / 40 | Section headlines. No hot word. |
| H3 | 19 / 22 | Tile and row headlines. |
| Claim | 19 / 22, weight 500 | Under the tagline. |
| Lead | 17 / 19 | Hero paragraph. |
| Body | 15 / 17 | Max 70 characters a line. |
| Small | 13 / 14 | |
| Label | 11, tracked 0.3em, uppercase, vermilion | Section labels. |
| Button | 12 / 13, tracked 0.14em, uppercase | |

Mono uppercase never runs longer than four words.

**Logo.** Headless match: stick and flame, no head. Wordmark FIREBUG in Space Mono bold, BUG always vermilion. Three lockups (horizontal, stacked, mark alone). Clear space one flame-width. The flame never recolors. On Soot, the stick goes Ash. One-color versions exist for ink and for white on a vermilion field. Files are attached; don't redraw.

**Grid.** Phone: 4 columns, 16 gutter, 20 margin. Desktop: 12 columns, 24 gutter, 48 margin, 1200 max. Running text never wider than 5 desktop columns. The hero figure takes 7 columns and bleeds off the right edge. Sections 96 apart on desktop, 64 on phone, with a hairline between. Spacing scale, base 4: 4, 8, 12, 16, 24, 32, 48, 64, 96, 128.

**Devices.** This is the whole kit. Nothing else.

- Hairline rules between sections and rows.
- The ground: a 1.5px Soot line once per page, under the hero, with the ember at its right end.
- The ember: a 7px vermilion dot. The only icon in the system. Marks the live thing: current nav item, selected option, end of the ground.
- Labels and two-digit numbers instead of icons.
- The hot word: last word of a Display or H1 line, vermilion.
- The band: one full-bleed Soot section per page at most. White headline, Ash body. Never holds an image. Home carries the argument in it; What we do the three levels; About the beliefs; Let's talk has none.
- Three buttons: vermilion fill, Soot outline, mono text with a vermilion underline.
- Links: ink with a vermilion underline.
- Fields: a single hairline under the label, vermilion on focus. No boxes.
- Images: on white with no frame, or full bleed, or replaced by a pull quote.

**Not in the system.** Icons, cards, drop shadows, gradients, rounded containers, decorative flames, stock photography, illustration styles other than the hero figure, dark mode.

## 4. The pages (content fixed, design open)

Four pages. Nav: What we do · About · Let's talk (button). Footer on every page: wordmark, seven disciplines (Brand and marketing strategy · Identity and design · Digital · Content marketing · Social media · Marketing systems · Public relations), email, LinkedIn, copyright. All copy is in the attached brand guidelines; use it verbatim.

**Home.** Hero (tagline, claim, lead paragraph, Let's talk, the figure, the ground). Argument band ("Fast and good. Pick both."). Four moments as numbered tiles under "If this is you, we should talk." Range: "One story, told in every language." with six discipline rows. A reserved, empty slot for proof. Close ("Make an impression."). Footer.

**What we do.** Hero ("One story, told in every language." plus core message two). Three levels band ("The brand. The story. The reach."). Seven disciplines as rows with belief line and service list; Marketing systems expands into "Build it. Run it. Or both." Four moments in full. Close. Footer.

**About.** Hero ("Small on purpose." plus the plain answer). Beliefs band (Plutarch epigraph, four beliefs). Who you'll work with (portrait, Chris in third person; bio pending). How it works (four steps). Close. Footer.

**Let's talk.** Hero ("Start with a conversation."). Form: name, email, organization, "Which sounds most like you?" with the four moments and "Something else," message, Send. Direct: email, LinkedIn. Footer. No close block.

The approved wireframes and a first literal rendering of Home in the system are attached. The rendering is a floor, not a target: it's what the system produces when applied without design judgment. Beat it.

## 5. What we want explored

In this order. Each is a decision we'll make from what you show.

1. **The hero figure.** A person touching a match to the ground and the fire taking off. Stencil or street-art register, our own figure and hand. Fire reads as spread and momentum, never destruction. "Logo at rest, hero in motion." We want to see this as a short motion piece (a loop of a few seconds: match down, fire takes) and as its static frame for reduced motion. Show two or three directions. We'll decide format (vector animation vs rendered video), length and weight after seeing them. This is the one place on the site where the identity gets to be expressive.
2. **Home, desktop and phone.** The whole page with the figure in it. The rhythm from hero to band to tiles to close. Where the page breathes and where it doesn't.
3. **The three inner pages.** What we do first (the longest, and the one with the most rows), then About, then Let's talk.
4. **The portrait treatment** for About: how a photograph of one person sits in a system with no frames and no photography elsewhere.

Not asked for: new pages, new copy, a new mark, a color beyond the palette, icons, a dark mode, a mobile menu pattern beyond a plain list.

---

## 6. Deliverables and handoff

- Artboards at 1440 and 390 for each page, in the order above.
- The hero figure directions, with a note on how each would be produced.
- Any new spacing or type value, called out, so it can go into the tokens. The expectation is none.
- The build is Next.js from the existing repository. Design decisions come back as values in the system, not as exported images, except the figure itself.
